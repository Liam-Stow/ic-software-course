Feedforward & feedback control

~15 min · concepts
🎯

By the end

You'll understand why "80% power" isn't good enough for a shooter, and what feedforward and feedback (PID) control actually do.

This lesson is all concepts — no typing. Take your time; everything in Module 4 builds on these ideas.

The problem with "just set 80% power"

In Module 1 we set the shooter to 80% power. But power is not the same as speed. The actual flywheel speed at 80% power drifts depending on conditions:

  • When the battery gets low, the same 80% delivers less, so the wheel slows.
  • When other subsystems draw power, the flywheel slows.
  • Right after a ball is fired, the wheel has slowed and needs to recover.

For a shooter this is a big deal: a slower wheel means the ball flies less far and misses the target. We don't want "about 80% power" — we want an exact, held speed, say 1000 RPM, no matter what.

Feedforward — a smart first guess

Feedforward predicts how much voltage should get the wheel to the target speed, based on a model of the motor. It does most of the work up front. The main term we tune is V (the velocity feedforward gain): roughly, "how much voltage per unit of speed". A good feedforward gets you very close to target on its own.

Feedback (PID) — correcting the error

No model is perfect, so we add feedback: the controller measures the actual speed, compares it to the target, and nudges the voltage to close the gap. This is the famous PID controller. Its three gains:

  • P (proportional) — push harder the further you are from target. The main one you'll tune.
  • I (integral) — corrects small, persistent errors that build up over time.
  • D (derivative) — damps overshoot by reacting to how fast the error is changing.

Put together, feedforward + PID is often written PIDF.

The recipe we'll follow

For a well-made flywheel, a great tuning recipe is: set everything to zero, raise V until you reach the speed you want, then raise P until it gets there quickly without overshooting or oscillating. I and D can usually stay at zero for a flywheel. You'll do exactly this in Lesson 19.

? Why is setting "80% power" not enough for a shooter?
The same power gives different speeds as the battery drains or other systems draw current. A shooter needs a held, exact speed, which means controlling speed directly — not power.
? What's the difference between feedforward and feedback?
Feedforward is the educated first guess from a model; feedback (PID) watches the real measurement and trims the error the model missed. Used together they hold a target tightly.
