Ninja Code: going further

Module 7 ~10 min
🎯

By the end

You'll know what to learn next and exactly where to find it — the big topics that turn a working robot into a competitive one.

You've built subsystems, commands, PID control, simulations and automated sequences — the core of FRC programming. The topics below are very common but beyond the scope of this guide. Each has links to dive deeper, and self-research is part of the challenge. Importantly, plenty of the IC Software team know these well and can teach you one-on-one — ask.

🛞 Making a swerve drive base

Swerve lets the robot drive in any direction while facing any way — the standard for competitive FRC. It's a big step up: many motors, modules, and odometry.

  • WPILib SwerveDrivePoseEstimator example (C++) ↗
  • IC 2024 GAUSS — SubDrivebase.h ↗
  • IC 2024 GAUSS — SubDrivebase.cpp ↗
  • IC 2024 GAUSS — SwerveModule.h ↗
  • IC 2024 GAUSS — SwerveModule.cpp ↗
📍 Knowing where you are on the field

Field localisation (pose estimation) makes it possible to aim at and drive to arbitrary points — invaluable in both autonomous and teleop.

  • WPILib pose-estimator example ↗
  • PhotonVision — adding vision measurements ↗
🤖 Autonomous

The first 15 seconds of every match run with no driver. Good autonomous routines win games.

  • Zero to Autonomous YouTube series ↗ (Java, but the theory carries to C++)
  • PathPlanner ↗ — the tool IC uses to generate autonomous paths
🎛️ WPILib advanced controls

Once PIDF feels comfortable, there's a whole world of state-space control, motion profiling and feedforward modelling.

  • Advanced Controls — WPILib documentation ↗

You went from installing WPILib to automating a shooter, turret, feeder and climber in simulation. That's a real, working command-based robot program. The next step is a real robot — bring these skills to the team and keep building.
